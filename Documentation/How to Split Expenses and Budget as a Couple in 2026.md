## **1\. Start With a Money Conversation**

• Do you prefer joint finances or separate finances?  
• How should large purchases work?  
• How should emergency spending work?

• How do you feel about debt?  
• How should bills be divided?

## **2\. Choose the Right Finance Structure**

Most couples use one of three setups.

### **Fully Joint Finances**

Everything stays shared:  
• Accounts  
• Budgets  
• Savings  
• Bills

This works well for couples with:  
• Shared long-term goals  
• Similar spending habits  
• Combined financial planning

The downside is less independence around personal spending.

### **Fully Separate Finances**

Each person manages their own money independently.

Bills get split manually.  
Transfers happen regularly.  
Personal spending stays private.

This setup creates more flexibility but also creates operational problems:  
• Shared expenses become harder to track  
• Reimbursements get forgotten  
• Nobody sees the full picture

### **Hybrid Finances**

This is one of the most common setups today.

Couples combine shared expenses while keeping personal spending separate.

Shared expenses usually include:  
• Rent  
• Utilities  
• Groceries  
• Travel  
• Subscriptions  
• Shared savings

Personal accounts still handle:  
• Shopping  
• Hobbies  
• Personal spending  
• Side income

This setup balances visibility with independence.

## **4\. Use a Budgeting System Both People Will Continue Using**

The best budgeting system is the one both people still use six months later.

Many couples stop budgeting because the process becomes too complicated.

Some common methods include:

### **Flexible Budgeting**

You track broad spending groups without strict category limits.

Good for couples who want:  
• Simplicity  
• Lower maintenance  
• Flexible spending

### **Envelope Budgeting**

Money gets divided into categories before spending happens.

Moneko uses Pockets for this workflow.

Examples include:  
• Groceries  
• Dining  
• Travel  
• Entertainment  
• Savings

Inside shared Spaces, couples create shared Pockets while still keeping personal spending separate.

This makes shared budgeting easier operationally.

### **Zero-Based Budgeting**

Every dollar receives a purpose.

This works well for:  
• Aggressive savings goals  
• Debt payoff  
• Tight cash flow management

Though maintenance becomes heavier over time.

Most couples do better with systems that reduce manual work.
