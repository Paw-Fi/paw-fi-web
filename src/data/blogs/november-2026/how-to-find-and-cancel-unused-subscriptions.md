# How to Find and Cancel Unused Subscriptions

To find unused subscriptions, review your bank and credit-card statements, Apple and Google Play subscription settings, payment accounts, and email receipts. Put every recurring charge on one list. For each service you no longer want, identify who bills you, cancel through the appropriate provider, save the confirmation, and check the next expected statement.

Four actions matter here. **Tracking** a charge makes it visible. **Cancelling the service** ends it under the provider’s terms. **Cancelling an automatic payment authorization** changes a merchant’s permission to charge through that payment method, but may not end the service agreement. **Checking the next statement** tells you whether another charge appeared. Treat each as a separate step.

## Find every recurring charge

Begin with the accounts you actually use. Review bank statements, credit-card transactions, and any shared household account that may pay for a service. Looking back 12 months is useful because an annual membership will not appear in a shorter review.

Flag charges that repeat monthly, quarterly, or yearly. A price may change after a trial, and a statement may show a payment processor rather than the service’s familiar name. Note unfamiliar charges for investigation; do not assume they are subscriptions or that they are authorized.

Next, check the accounts where subscriptions can be managed:

- **Apple:** On an iPhone, open **Settings → your name → Subscriptions**. Check any other Apple Account you may have used.
- **Google Play:** Open subscriptions while signed in to each relevant Google Account.
- **Payment services:** Review PayPal automatic payments and any other payment account you use.
- **Email:** Search for *receipt*, *invoice*, *renewal*, *trial*, and the merchant names on your statements. Check other inboxes used by your household if appropriate.

Match each charge to a service, an account owner, and a biller. An app you use on your phone may be billed directly by its provider, so it may not appear in Apple or Google Play settings. If a charge still cannot be identified, contact the provider or your payment institution through an official channel.

Not every recurring expense should be cancelled. Insurance, utilities, and loan payments may repeat. This audit identifies what you pay; the decision to end a service comes afterward.

## Make one audit record for each service

Use a note, spreadsheet, or paper list. These hypothetical entries show a compact way to compare services before deciding what to do:

| Service | Cost | Next renewal | Decision |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Music app | $9/month | 15 Jan 2027 | Cancel |
| Cloud storage | $14/month | 3 Feb 2027 | Review plan |
| Photo editing app | $60/year | 1 Jul 2027 | Keep |

For each service, also note the **biller** (the company or platform taking payment) and the **account** connected to the charge, such as an Apple Account, Google Account, PayPal account, provider login, or payment account. If you cancel, record the confirmation number or save the email or screenshot, note the effective date, and check the next expected statement to verify whether another charge appeared. Keep those details in your notes rather than adding more columns to the comparison table.

For services you keep, note the current price and next renewal. To plan for an annual charge, divide it by 12. A hypothetical $120 yearly membership represents $10 a month to set aside, although the provider will charge according to its actual billing schedule.

Before cancelling a shared service, check whether someone else uses it. If you use a subscription occasionally, compare its terms with any cheaper plan or pause option the provider offers. A pause is not the same as cancellation, and its effect on billing depends on the provider.

## Cancel through the correct biller

Use the biller in your audit record to choose the cancellation route. Deleting an app from your device does not cancel its subscription; [Google Play states this explicitly](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/7018481?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid.&hl=en).

### If Apple bills you

For a subscription bought from Apple or through the App Store, go to **Settings → your name → Subscriptions** on an iPhone. Select the active subscription, tap **Cancel Subscription**, and complete any confirmation steps. Apple’s [iPhone subscription guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/iph4e3e7324f/ios) documents this route.

If you cannot find the item, check another Apple Account and search your receipts to see which account made the purchase. A service bought directly from a company generally needs to be cancelled with that company, even if you use its app on an iPhone. Record the status and end date shown after cancellation.

### If Google Play bills you

Sign in to the Google Account used for the purchase and open your [Google Play subscriptions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/7018481?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid.&hl=en). Select the service, tap **Cancel subscription**, and follow the prompts. If it is missing, check another Google Account or ask the provider how you are billed.

Google says you can generally continue using a cancelled subscription for the time already paid. Continued access during that period does not by itself mean another renewal is scheduled. Check the status and end date for your particular plan.

### If you authorized PayPal automatic payments

PayPal’s [automatic-payment guide](https://www.paypal.com/us/cshelp/article/what-is-an-automatic-payment-and-how-do-i-update-or-cancel-one-help240) directs users to **Settings → Payments → Subscriptions and saved businesses** or **Automatic Payments**. Select the merchant to review or cancel its PayPal payment authorization.

Then check the **merchant’s service account**. Ending permission to charge through PayPal does not necessarily end a separate membership or contract with that merchant. Follow the merchant’s cancellation terms and keep its confirmation as well. In your audit notes, record both confirmations when applicable: one for the payment authorization and one for the service.

### If the provider bills you directly

Sign in through the provider’s official website or app and look under account, billing, plan, or subscription settings. Follow the process through its final confirmation screen. If you cannot find a cancellation route, contact the provider through its official support channel and ask for written confirmation and an effective date.

Check whether paid access ends immediately or at the close of the billing period. Do not assume cancellation brings a refund; the provider’s terms govern that question. Nor should you treat changing your card number or blocking a payment as proof that the underlying agreement ended.

## Verify that billing has stopped

After cancellation, update the audit record. Keep the confirmation, note the effective date, and mark when the next charge would otherwise have occurred. Check the provider account for its current status.

Review the relevant bank, card, or payment statement around that expected date. If another charge appears, compare its date with the cancellation terms. Contact the biller with your confirmation if the charge seems wrong. If the matter remains unresolved, ask your payment institution about its dispute process; procedures and deadlines vary by location and payment method.

This final check matters because a cancellation screen and a bank statement answer different questions. The first records what you requested. The second shows what was actually charged.

## A hypothetical $336 annual savings example

Suppose an audit finds three unwanted services:

| Service | Charge that would otherwise occur | Future charges avoided |
|---|---:|---:|
| Unused app | $9 monthly for 12 months | $108 |
| Duplicate streaming service | $14 monthly for 12 months | $168 |
| Annual membership | One $60 renewal | $60 |
| **Total** | | **$336** |

The assumptions are specific: each service is cancelled before its next charge; the two monthly prices remain unchanged; all 12 monthly charges would otherwise fall within the illustrated year; and the annual renewal also falls within that year. The arithmetic is **$9 × 12 + $14 × 12 + $60 = $336** in *future charges avoided*.

That is not an immediate refund or a prediction of anyone’s savings. A charge already paid is not recovered unless the provider refunds it. Your actual amount depends on renewal dates, prices, and any remaining obligations under the service terms.

## Reusable subscription audit checklist

- [ ] Review 12 months of relevant bank and card transactions; flag repeating charges and annual renewals.
- [ ] Check subscriptions under each relevant Apple and Google Account.
- [ ] Review PayPal automatic payments and other payment accounts you use.
- [ ] Search receipts and renewal emails to identify each service and its biller.
- [ ] Complete an audit record and decide whether to keep, change, cancel, or investigate each service.
- [ ] Cancel unwanted services through the actual provider or app store; separately end a payment authorization where appropriate.
- [ ] Save confirmations and effective dates.
- [ ] Check the next expected statement for another charge.
- [ ] Budget for subscriptions you choose to keep.

## Keep the subscriptions you retain visible

Review the price and renewal date of each active service when you review your budget. For an annual charge, setting aside part of the cost each month can make the renewal easier to plan for. Revisit a service when its price or your use of it changes.

Moneko’s [Recurring feature](https://www.moneko.io/help/recurring-expenses-income-moneko) helps you keep regular subscription costs visible in your budget, while a [Subscriptions Pocket](https://www.moneko.io/help/how-to-use-pockets-to-organize-your-spending) lets you monitor what you’ve planned and spent in that category. Recording renewal dates and reviewing those expenses can make upcoming charges easier to plan for. When you decide to end a subscription, complete the cancellation through the service provider or billing platform and check your statement afterward.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is a charge missing from Apple or Google Play subscriptions?

It may belong to another account, be billed directly by the provider, or use a different payment service. Match the statement date and amount to receipts before choosing where to cancel.

### Does cancelling an automatic payment cancel the service?

Not necessarily. A payment authorization and a service agreement can be separate. Confirm cancellation with the service provider, even if you also end its automatic payment authorization.

### Should I wait until an annual plan renews to cancel it?

Check the provider’s terms and the access end date shown in your account. Some cancellations stop the next renewal while access continues through the paid period. Save the confirmation and check that the next expected charge does not occur.
